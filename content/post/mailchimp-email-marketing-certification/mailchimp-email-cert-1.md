# Mailchimp Email Marketing Certification - Learning Note

## Planning Phase of Any Email Marketing
Planning is the most important part in any email campaign. There's 4 aspects in planning phase: 
1. Understanding Your Audience
2. Setting Your Marketing Objective
3. Building Your Email (Blueprint)
4. Quality Assurance & Testing

---
### 1) Understanding Your Audience
To understand your audience, you first must **==Build==** a High-Quality Audience List.

A High-Quality Audience List is:
* **Updated:** It's always a work-in-progress because people are constant and evolving.
* **Data-Rich**
* **Organized:** 
	- Via **Tags, Groups, Segments**
	- Think in **Demographics, Behaviors, Interests**

#### Best practices for building & maintaining the list:
* **Use a Master Audience**
* **Maintain Proper Permission**
* **Re-engage or Unsubscribe Inactive contacts:**
  * *Periodically reconfirm their interest* in receiving emails.
  * Otherwise *archive* or *unsubscribe* them.
* **Allows Contacts to Update their Profiles:**
  * Include an update profile link so recipients can update their personal information + preferences + interests (can be done via MailChimp function).
  * Or using a self-made form to update: 5-10 questions per survey, in a friendly, non-invasive way.
* **Consider your Opt-In Method:**
	* 
	  * **Single Opt-In**: Subscribe with just one step/click -> added to the list immediately with no notification.
	  * **Double Opt-In:** Subscribe by confirming the signup via email/verification email.
	* Consider the consents:
	  * **Implied/Implicit** Consent: Where they gave you the email for some business purposes but has not explicitly stated that they want to receive marketing emails.
	  * **Explicit** Consent: Where you ask and they agreed via manually opt-in.
* **Use reCAPTCHA Subscribe Confirmation:**
  * [Prevent Fake Signups](https://mailchimp.com/help/about-fake-signups/) because Spambots who sign up automatically will submit fake information and even click links inside emails, so it skews the accuracy of your audience statistics.

> [!quote] Remember you are not a list builder, you are a marketer.

### 2) Setting Your Marketing Objective

#### Define the Business/Marketing Objectives

Starting campaigns with clear objectives ensures every email supports your goals. Use these objectives to choose testing methods and define success KPIs.

- **Periodic Check-ins:** Regularly review performance to optimize results or make necessary adjustments.
   - examples of objectives:
	   - Revenue Growth
	   - Subscribe Growth
	   - Sales threshold for New Product


#### Decide on Email Marketing Type

**A. Behavior-Based Emails**
Automated emails triggered by specific recipient actions and responses.
> **Best Practices:** Be creative, narrow your list to the best options, and continuously optimize.
- **Purchase Behavior**
    - Order Confirmation & Cross-Sell
- **Campaign Engagement**
    - Win-Back / Re-engagement Email
- **App Engagement**
    - Onboarding / Feature Adoption Guide
- **Website Engagement*
    - Browse Abandonment Follow-up
- **Customer Loyalty**
    - VIP Reward Notification

**B. Campaign-Based Emails**
Scheduled or manual broadcasts integrated with other marketing channels to build a cohesive, multi-touchpoint customer journey.

### 3) Building Your Email (Blueprint)

#### The WHY
- Who is your Target Audience?
- What are you Marketing Objectives (Behavior-Based, Campaign Based, KPIs)?
- What is a 1-2 Sentence Description of an Email that Meet These Objectives?

#### The BUILD

##### 5 Elements of an Email
| "Build" Element                     | Description | Testing Consideration |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------- | --------------------- |
| **Frequency** + **Timing**          |             |                       |
| **Message Structure**               |             |                       |
| **Subject Lines**, **Preview Text** |             |                       |
| **Content**, **Copy**, **Images**   |             |                       |
| Calls to Action (**CTAs**)          |             |                       |

1) **Frequency + Timing**
   - Keep track of marketing activities and email schedules with a calendar.
   - Send at least **1 email per month**.
   - Put yourself in your audience's shoes.

2) **Message Structure**
   - **Lead with your Main Point**
     - Start with the conclusion, then support it with key points.
     - If you have multiple messages, split them into a series of emails.
     - If there's lots of content, keep the email concise and use the CTA to drive readers to your website.
   - **Keep it Organized for Quick Reading**
     - If an email can't be read quickly, it probably won't be read.
     - Break text into bite-sized sections.
     - Keep it short and prioritize the most important information at the top.
     - Use **14–16px** font size and limit yourself to **2–3 fonts**.
   - **Maintain a Smooth Logic Flow**
     - Ideas should flow naturally (A → B → C → D), not jump around.
     - Poor flow can result from unorganized writing, coding issues, or device compatibility.
     - Ensure content is displayed in a logical order on all devices, especially for screen reader users.
   - **Build with Mobile in Mind**
     - Around **40%** of emails are opened on mobile devices.
     - Many users preview emails on mobile before reading them on desktop.

3) **Subject Lines, Preview Text**
   - **Personalize**
   	- Use recipients' names sparingly.
   	- Tie subject lines to recipients' [[Demographics, Behaviors, Interests (Segmentation)|Demographics, Behaviors, Interests]].
   	- Avoid deceptive personalization.
   - **Clarity beats Ambiguity**
   	- Be clear and descriptive while still communicating the benefit.
   	- Avoid ALL CAPS.
   	- According to Mailchimp, subject lines that sound like requests tend to have lower open rates.
   - **Strength over Length**
   	- No ideal character count—test what works.
   	- Long subject lines may be cropped on mobile or with larger font settings.
   	- Word Pairings can be Impactful both Positively & Negatively 
   	  - negative - (e.g., *Sign Up*, *Last Chance*).
   - **Use Emojis Carefully**
   	- They help emails stand out but render differently across platforms.
   - **The "From" Name Matters**
   	- Test different sender names (company, full name, first name, etc.).
   - Prepare **3–5** subject lines → Test **2** → Use the best **1**.

4) **Content, Copy, Images**
   - Strategy should drive content, copy, and images.
   - Every element should support the marketing objective.

    - **Content**
      - Since creating multiple versions is expensive, prioritize testing other elements.
    - **Copy**
      - Test different hooks, short vs. long copy, or serious vs. humorous tone.
    - **Images**
      - Test image vs. no image, large vs. small, placement, different crops, or many images vs. one.

5) **Calls to Action (CTAs)**

   - **Make it Simple**
     - One action → one result.
     - If using multiple CTAs, clearly distinguish them and make the primary CTA most prominent.
   - **Make it Important**
     - Optimize button placement and design to look important.
   - **Make it Clear**
     - Set clear expectations about where the CTA leads.

   - A/B Testing Ideas
     - Green vs. blue button.
     - Button vs. clickable product image.
     - Round button vs. seasonal shapes.
     - CTA at the top vs. bottom.
     - Wording changes (e.g., "Supplies Are Limited" vs. "Limited Time Offer").

### 4) Quality Assurance & Testing

#### The TESTING

Use **A/B** and **Multivariate Testing** on the **5 Elements of an Email**.
- Testing should be planned from the beginning, not treated as an afterthought.
- Every campaign should have a *testing strategy* before the first email is sent (e.g., subject lines, images, CTAs, etc.) and how often it'll be tested.

#### 6-Step Email QA Process

- **Review subject line and preview text**
  - Check for cropped subject lines.
  - Verify emoji rendering.
  - Compare across browsers and devices.

- **Check the email on different devices and browsers**
  - Test on multiple email clients and providers.
  - Review on mobile (iPhone, Android) and desktop.

- **Verify images**
  - Ensure images aren't squished, pixelated, or poorly cropped.
  - Optimize file size for faster loading.
  - Confirm alt text is included.

- **Proofread content**
  - Check spelling, grammar, and punctuation.
  - Avoid incorrect titles (Mr., Mrs., Ms., etc.) by using neutral greetings like "Hi John,".
  - Be mindful of cultural and holiday diversity.

- **Confirm all links work**
  - Verify every link points to the correct destination.
  - Test all links, including CTA, unsubscribe, social, preference center, and terms.

- **Verify the audience segment**
  - Ensure the email is sent to the correct audience.
  - Even a perfect email fails if it reaches the wrong recipients.

## Post-Send Phase of MailChimp Email Marketing

### Metrics to Look For
- **Open Rate**
  - ``Numbers of Opens / Total Received``
- **Click-Through Rate**
	- The Effectiveness of CTA (in total)
	-  ``Numbers of CTA Clicked / Total Received``
- **Clicks per Unique Open** // **Clicks to Open Rate (CTOR)**
  - The Effectiveness of CTA (in opens)
  - `` Numbers of CTA Clicked / Numbers of Opens``
	  - > If you sent 300 emails which were opened by 100 contacts, then 60 of those who opened it clicked the CTA, your clicks per unique open is 60%.
- **Conversion Rate**
  - Percentage of People Completed a Desired Action (Marketing Objectives like Purchase and etc)
- **Total Revenue**
  - OR **Average Spend per Customer**
  - tells how much extra income was generated for your client as a direct result of your email campaign. 
- **Unsubscribe Rate**
  - Indicator of:
	  - Content Irrelevance
	  - Audience Satisfaction & Trust
	  - Frequency
- **Hard Bounce & Soft Bounce**
- **[Click Map / Heat Map](https://mailchimp.com/help/about-click-maps/)**
- **Promo Code Usage**
- **Read Time**
