## Key Learnings

### 1. Planning Comes Before Everything

The biggest takeaway from this course is that successful email campaigns begin long before you write the email.

In summary, I learned how to:

- Build and maintain a healthy email list.
- Plan campaign objectives **together with** the metrics needed to measure success, instead of only thinking about the end goal.
- Segment audiences by demographics, behaviors, and interests, while organizing them using tags, groups, and segments.
- Define A/B testing and QA strategies early, rather than treating them as an afterthought.

<hr>

### 2. Best Practices for Building & Maintaining an Audience

One lesson I found particularly useful was that **you are a marketer, not a list builder**.

Instead of trying to grow the biggest email list possible, the goal is to maintain a healthy and trusting audience that actually wants to hear from you.

A few best practices that stood out to me:

- **Keep a master audience.** Avoid creating multiple lists for different occasions, as this leads to duplicated contacts and makes audience management harder.
- **Always collect proper consent.** After all, nobody likes unsolicited marketing emails. Depending on your use case, you should also consider whether explicit or implicit consent, as well as single or double opt-in, makes the most sense.
- **Prevent fake signups.** Bots can crawl your website, discover your signup form, and submit fake information. This pollutes your audience data, skews campaign reporting, and can even trigger fake email interactions. Verifying email addresses helps keep your audience list clean.
- **Re-engage inactive subscribers.** Sometimes people simply lose interest, while others may stop seeing your emails because they land in spam or hidden folders. Sending a re-engagement campaign helps identify subscribers who still want to hear from you before removing inactive contacts.

Speaking of inactive subscribers, one thing I also learned outside of the certification is about **spam traps**. 

An email address that was once legitimate can eventually be abandoned and recycled by an ISP into a spam trap. Sending emails to even one spam trap can seriously damage your sender reputation and email deliverability. 

That's why **regularly cleaning your email list**—not just for inactive subscribers, but also for fake or bot-generated addresses—is more important than simply growing your subscriber count.

<hr>

### 3. Building Effective Emails

I also learnt how to think of emails, in terms of the **five elements of an email**, along with best practices and ideas for testing each one:

- Frequency & Timing
- Message Structure
- Subject Lines & Preview Text
- Content, Copy & Images
- Calls-to-Action (CTAs)

Some of the tips I found most useful were:

- **Build with mobile users in mind.** Many people check emails on their phones first, so emails should be designed with mobile in mind. Images should scale properly, text should be easy to read, and the overall layout should be quick to scan.
- **Avoid titles like Mr., Mrs., Ms., or Sir** unless you're absolutely certain they're correct. A simple "Hi Sin Yin," is usually safer and avoids accidentally misgendering someone.
- **Never use misleading subject lines.** I always feel disappointed when I open an email expecting one thing, only to find a hard sales pitch. The course repeatedly emphasizes being honest and thinking from your audience's perspective, which I completely agree with.

<hr>

### 4. Quality Assurance & A/B Testing

Testing should be planned **before** the campaign is created, not just before pressing **Send**.

Since creating multiple versions of different email elements requires different amounts of time and effort, it's important to plan your testing strategy early. For example, producing different versions of email content can be much more resource-intensive than testing subject lines, images, or CTA buttons.

One recommendation I really liked was:

    Prepare 3–5 subject lines → Test 2 → Send the best 1

    For example, the subject lines themselves can experiment with different approaches
    - like being straightforward versus asking a question, 
    - using the recipient's first name, or last name, versus no name
    - mentioning a demographic (e.g. Mothers), or a specific audience segment (e.g. Cat owners). 
  
    There are so many fun variations you can test before settling on the one that performs best!

Another useful takeaway was to **test one variable at a time** whenever possible. Comparing two CTA buttons gives much clearer insights than changing the copy, images, and layout all at once. After all, A/B testing isn't just about improving one campaign—it's about learning what works so future campaigns become even better.

I also learned the **6-step Email QA checklist**, which covers everything from checking links and images to reviewing audience segments before launching a campaign.

<hr>

### 5. Measuring Campaign Performance

This section was fairly straightforward. The certification introduced the key metrics used to evaluate email marketing campaigns, including:

- Open Rate
- Click-Through Rate (CTR)
- Click-to-Open Rate (CTOR)
- Conversion Rate
- Revenue
- Soft & Hard Bounce
- Unsubscribe Rate

These metrics help determine whether a campaign is:
- achieving its marketing objectives 
- highlighting the potential issues such as poor engagement, deliverability problems (e.g., Soft & Hard Bounce Rate), or content that isn't resonating with the audience ()

## Conclusion

Overall, I enjoyed this certification. It's beginner-friendly, practical, and focuses on the fundamentals of email marketing rather than just teaching Mailchimp's features. If you're looking to learn the platform itself in greater depth, Mailchimp Academy also offers the **[Mailchimp Foundations Certification](https://www.mailchimpacademy.com/student/path/469473-mailchimp-foundations-certification?)**, which I also completed. That certification focuses more on using Mailchimp's tools and features, making the two certifications complement each other well.

If you're new to email marketing like I am, I'd definitely recommend this certification as a great starting point before diving into more advanced topics such as automation, customer journeys, and lifecycle marketing.
