## What I Learned from the Tutorial

Here are the key takeaways that reshaped the way I see data cleaning and analysis:

1. **Evaluate the Importance of Issues**  
   I used to think every problem in the dataset had to be fixed. Christine taught me to focus on **issues that truly impact the analysis**:  
   - Ignore problems that are irrelevant  
   - Leave unsolvable issues as they are, unless external data is provided  
   - Escalate corrections to the right team instead of patching everything yourself  

2. **Organized and Documented Cleaning Process**  
   Christine’s workflow is **structured and collaborative**:  
   - Duplicate columns for cleaning instead of touching the original  
   - Keep **side-by-side sheets** for easy comparison  
   - Maintain a **log sheet** noting issues, their magnitude, and how (or if) they were resolved  

   This approach makes work **transparent**, easier to **collaborate on**, and **less reliant on memory**, saving mental energy over time.

3. **Use Sparklines for Quick Insights**  
   Adding *sparklines* to tables or pivot tables provides a **quick visual sense of trends, anomalies, and outliers**.  
   - Helps during **data cleaning** to spot unexpected values  
   - Supports **analysis** by revealing seasonal trends or unusual spikes without building full charts  

4. **Christine Jiang’s Frameworks for Structured, Defensible Work**  
   Christine created the **CLEAN** and **SCAN** frameworks to bring **business logic and stakeholder focus** into data analysis:  
   - **CLEAN Framework** – guides structured data cleaning with clear reasoning  
   - **SCAN Framework** – structures exploratory data analysis around **metrics, anomalies, and notable segments**  
   - **Observation Log** – document every decision, solution, and recommendation, making your work **transparent and defensible**  

   Her methods make data work **grounded in business logic**, **collaborative**, and **easier to defend** when questioned.  
   While this approach takes **more effort upfront**, it ultimately saves **time and brainpower** and makes teamwork smoother.

## Conclusion

This tutorial gave me a **professional perspective on data analysis**. It’s not just about fixing data—it’s about **understanding the context, documenting your reasoning, and making analysis actionable and defensible**.  

If you want **structured, step-by-step guidance with clear explanations for every decision**, I **highly recommend Christine Jiang’s tutorials**. They’re practical, organized, and teach skills that are directly relevant to real-world data analysis.
